How the assessment works

This is a claims-risk scan, not a broad ESG rating. It identifies sustainability wording that may be too broad, insufficiently evidenced, consumer-facing under EmpCo, exposed to the EU Forced Labour Regulation, or potentially contradicted by relevant external public-source context.

  1. Claim wording: broad, generic, absolute, future-oriented, comparative or label/icon-based claims.
  2. Evidence gap: missing scope, methodology, KPIs, limits, verification, grievance/remedy, audit coverage, traceability or due-diligence evidence.
  3. External context: relevant public signals that may contradict or qualify the claim.
  4. Regulatory lens: EmpCo (Directive (EU) 2024/825, "Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition") for consumer-facing green claims; the EU Forced Labour Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/3015) for forced-labour and supply-chain claims.

Key sustainability claim signals

This scan is built around two EU legal lenses. EmpCo (Directive (EU) 2024/825) is primarily an environmental-claims law, with a narrower, secondary basis for social claims (Article 6(1)(b)); social signals under EmpCo are therefore limited to the most material, high-severity items. The EU Forced Labour Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/3015) covers forced-labour and supply-chain claims specifically. Claims that fit neither lens are shown separately.
